\section{Discussion and Analysis}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Edge Feasibility and Hardware Profiling}
A central design goal of Dioptra-DINO is practical feasibility on embedded robotics hardware. While most state-of-the-art spatial AI models assume cloud GPU servers or high-wattage workstations (e.g., NVIDIA RTX 4090 or A100), Dioptra-DINO executes steady-state neural forward inference in \textbf{$26.3 \pm 1.5$\,ms} ($38.0 \pm 2.2$\,FPS) and complete end-to-end perception pipeline processing (including frame unprojection, ray FiLM generation, model forward pass, and memory synchronization) in \textbf{$35.3 \pm 1.8$\,ms} ($\approx \mathbf{28.3 \pm 1.4}$\,FPS) on consumer Apple Silicon (\textbf{Apple M3}, 8-core CPU, 10-core GPU, 8\,GB unified memory, PyTorch 2.8.0 Metal Performance Shaders, unquantized FP32, batch size 1, evaluated over 100 repeated trials; p95 latency is $29.1$\,ms). The 14-pixel patch tokenization of DINOv2-Small ($16 \times 16 = 256$ tokens) drastically suppresses quadratic self-attention memory overhead, operating with $9.4\times$ less attention compute than patch-8 baselines.

Crucially, Dioptra-DINO's single-image inference consumes under \textbf{$210$\,MB of working RAM} (with total peak VRAM $<1.5$\,GB), while its complete model parameter footprint is only \textbf{$105.05$\,MB} in FP32 format ($52.5$\,MB in FP16). While evaluated on Apple Silicon as a representative low-power unified memory system ($15\text{--}20$\,W TDP), this compact profile translates directly to embedded robotics SoCs (such as NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano / AGX) without exhausting shared system memory.

\subsection{Surface Reconstruction and Normal Consistency}
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:visual_predictions} and Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_comparison}, depth predictions from Dioptra-DINO produce sharp, consistent 3D surface geometry. In our framework, Multi-Scale Virtual Normal Loss (VNL) functions as an architectural geometric regularizer during training: by enforcing planar collinearity across randomly sampled 3D point triplets over dilated spatial stencils ($\mathcal{S} \in \{1, 2, 4\}$), it suppresses high-frequency depth rippling and wall bending without requiring an auxiliary normal estimation subnetwork.

As reported in Table~\ref{tab:pointcloud_normals}, evaluating surface normals across structured trajectory keyframes yields a Mean Angular Error (MAE) of $25.56^\circ$ ($61.92\%$ within $22.5^\circ$). When evaluated across the complete, unbroken 200-frame continuous trajectory—which traverses complex untextured debris, broken rubble, and curved machinery where finite-difference discrete normal extraction degrades—the full-sequence median 3D angular error is \textbf{$39.80^\circ$}, with planar warehouse corridors reaching \textbf{$12.08^\circ$}. This reflects the inherent nature of finite-difference normal estimation on high-frequency discrete surfaces: VNL serves as an effective multi-scale regularizer that prevents low-frequency planar bowing and warping along architectural walls and floors, rather than functioning as an explicit semantic surface normal predictor.

\subsection{The Metric-Visual Paradox: Photometric Boundary Sharpness vs. Physical Geometric Accuracy}
\label{sec:metric_visual_paradox}
A critical empirical question arises when inspecting qualitative predictions: \textit{Why can foundation models such as Depth Anything V2~\cite{yang2024depth2} or Metric3D~\cite{yin2023metric3d} appear visually sharper along high-frequency silhouettes, yet exhibit significantly inferior metric benchmark scores compared to Dioptra-DINO?}

As demonstrated in Figure~\ref{fig:error_heatmaps_comparison}, this phenomenon stems from a fundamental divergence between \textbf{2D photometric edge contrast} and \textbf{3D physical metric calibration}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High-Frequency Edge Contrast vs. Physical Distance Accuracy}: Models pretrained on massive uncalibrated web datasets (e.g., Depth Anything V2 on 62M images) prioritize sharp gradient boundaries along semantic contours (window mullions, thin railings, and foreground obstacles) via high-frequency gradient losses. While human visual perception instinctively associates high edge contrast with reconstruction quality, standard metric robotics benchmarks (AbsRel, RMSE, $\delta_1$) measure true physical distance in metres. If a model predicts a sharp railing at $18$\,m when its ground-truth distance is $12$\,m, the prediction appears visually crisp, but incurs a large $50\%$ relative error ($0.50$ AbsRel). Conversely, Dioptra-DINO predicts the railing at $12.04$\,m ($0.003$ AbsRel), even if the boundary is slightly softer due to patch tokenization.
    \item \textbf{Token Resolution Budget ($256$ vs.\ $1{,}369$ / $3{,}344$ Tokens)}: Dioptra-DINO operates at $224 \times 224$ input resolution ($16 \times 16 = 256$ tokens) to adhere to embedded micro-robotics compute envelopes ($38.0$\,FPS, $26.3$\,ms latency). In contrast, Depth Anything V2 operates at $518 \times 518$ ($1{,}369$ tokens) and Metric3D processes $616 \times 1064$ inputs ($3{,}344$ tokens). The higher token density naturally yields finer 2D pixel-level edge delineations, but at severe computational expense ($1.8\text{--}8.6$\,FPS), rendering them unsuitable for real-time edge flight.
    \item \textbf{Colormap Dynamic Compression and Perceptual Contrast}: Metric3D suffers from severe scale underestimation under 6-DoF flight ($0.6843$ scale ratio, $-31.6\%$ scale bias). In non-linear colormaps (e.g., \texttt{plasma} or \texttt{magma}), compressing a $30$\,m scene into a compressed $15$\,m numerical range forces foreground and midground surfaces across high-gradient color bands. This artificial color contrast is perceptually misinterpreted by the human eye as ``richer depth variation,'' whereas it in fact reflects massive metric scale error.
    \item \textbf{Error Heatmap Disambiguation}: When examining absolute physical error heatmaps ($|\hat{\mathbf{D}} - \mathbf{D}^*|$, bottom row of Figure~\ref{fig:error_heatmaps_comparison}), the perceptual illusion dissolves: Dioptra-DINO remains uniformly dark blue ($\text{MAE} = 0.65$\,m, AbsRel $0.0555$), whereas Metric3D glows brightly with severe global errors ($\text{MAE} = 24.96$\,m), UniDepth-V2 exhibits localized planar warps ($\text{MAE} = 1.98$\,m), and Depth Anything V2 shows systematic depth drift ($\text{MAE} = 1.42$\,m).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Failure Mode and Operational Boundary Analysis}
Analyzing challenging regimes across our benchmark evaluations reveals distinct physical boundaries and failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Low-Light and Synthetic Fog (\textit{abandonedfactory\_night})}: In environments with extreme darkness and heavy synthetic fog, visual contrast degrades significantly. Here, AbsRel increases to $0.24$ and threshold accuracy drops to $69\%$ (scale ratio $0.94$). While structural silhouettes of buildings and shipping containers remain discernible, fine depth gradation within shadows is attenuated.
    \item \textbf{Severe Out-of-Distribution Shift (\textit{hospital})}: When tested on textureless clinical indoor corridors without fine-tuning, the model exhibits a scale over-prediction (AbsRel $1.33$, $\delta_1 \approx 3\%$, scale ratio $2.18$). This confirms that while ray conditioning generalizes across industrial and warehouse domains, transferring to completely textureless interior spaces requires multi-domain diversity during pretraining.
    \item \textbf{Foreground Clutter Dominance}: When the camera tilts steeply downward facing an obstacle within $<0.8$\,m that occupies $>60\%$ of the visual frame, image median anchoring shifts abruptly toward the near plane, amplifying relative error on distant background slivers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Camera-Intrinsic Equivariance vs. Positional Embedding}
\label{sec:fov_discussion}
A fundamental question for ray-conditioned networks~\cite{facil2019camconvs,yin2023metric3d,piccinelli2024unidepth} is whether explicit camera parameters $\mathbf{K}$ provide actionable geometric guidance or merely act as a passive coordinate bias. When trained exclusively on fixed-sensor datasets, models can overfit to the static relationship between image coordinates $(u, v)$ and ray vectors $\hat{\mathbf{r}}(u, v)$, rendering the ray embedding functionally equivalent to standard sinusoidal positional encoding. 

Our dynamic pinhole augmentation uncouples pixel space from metric ray directions by introducing stochastic window cropping ($L \in [0.55\cdot S_{\max}, S_{\max}]$) and continuous focal/center recalculations. As demonstrated in Table~\ref{tab:multi_fov_matrix}, conditioning depth decoding on dynamically varying $\hat{\mathbf{r}}(u, v)$ encourages the network to internalize optical equivariance:
\begin{equation}
    f_\theta(\mathbf{I} \circ \mathcal{T}_s, \mathbf{K}') \approx f_\theta(\mathbf{I}, \mathbf{K})
\end{equation}
where physical 3D scene distances remain invariant under optical focal scaling. The dynamic pinhole formulation substantially mitigates scale drift across varying optical configurations (maintaining metric scale ratio within $[-1.4\%, +3.0\%]$ of unity across $73.7^\circ \text{--} 100^\circ$ FOVs on the multi-frame benchmark).

\subsection{Limitations and Scope of Claims}
We explicitly delineate the methodological scope and operational boundaries of our study:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single-Trajectory Continuous Benchmark Foundation}: While our continuous 200-frame sequence in \textit{abandonedfactory/Easy/P010} provides dense temporal fidelity for evaluating inter-frame jitter, scale drift, and decorrelated keyframe hypothesis testing (Table~\ref{tab:statistical_significance}), all primary headline metrics derive from a single continuous flight in one synthetic facility. While multi-environment stress probes (Section~\ref{sec:multienv_generalization}) evaluate other conditions, multi-trajectory benchmarking across multiple independent simulation engines and physical flight spaces is necessary to establish unconditional population validity.
    \item \textbf{Single-Seed Retraining of Ablation Controls}: While a fixed seed ($42$) and deterministic cuDNN settings were enforced across all retrained ablation variants (Section~\ref{sec:training_details}), each ablation condition was trained once due to computational resource constraints. Consequently, while catastrophic degradation (such as the $0.0555 \to 0.5516$ AbsRel collapse when disabling ARA) reflects an unambiguous architectural failure to learn valid metric scaling, single-seed runs cannot isolate architectural effect sizes from seed-specific training variance across repeated initializations.
    \item \textbf{Foundation Baseline Scope and Domain Priors}: While our comparative evaluation in Table~\ref{tab:baseline_comparison} directly benchmarks against Metric3D ViT-Small and Depth Anything V2-Small, foundation models benefit from massive internet-scale pretraining corpora containing tens to hundreds of millions of diverse real-world images. Furthermore, Metric3D is optimized for level, upright autonomous driving and indoor tripod captures; testing on agile 6-DoF drone flights introduces non-horizontal pitch and roll angles ($>20^\circ$) that lie outside its canonical training distributions. Additionally, Metric3D requires high input resolution ($616 \times 1064$) for its RAFT decoder, whereas Dioptra-DINO is tailored specifically for compact $224 \times 224$ edge robotics. Our study isolates how explicit 3D camera ray conditioning and angular residual attention enable a compact model trained exclusively on synthetic stereo data to maintain physical metric scale on dynamic drone trajectories without requiring test-time alignment.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Pinhole Frustum Cropping vs. Physical Lens Distortion}: The camera equivariance benchmark in Section~\ref{sec:fov_equivariance} evaluates mathematical equivariance under synthetic projective cropping and focal adjustments following the ideal pinhole camera model ($\mathbf{K}'$). Real physical wide-angle and fisheye lenses introduce non-linear radial and tangential lens distortions ($k_1, k_2, p_1, p_2$), optical vignetting, and chromatic aberration that deviate from ideal projective pinhole unprojection. Validating equivariance across physical optical hardware with varying physical focal lengths and optical barrel distortions remains an essential boundary for hardware deployment.
    \item \textbf{Small-Sample Boundary Probes ($N \le 8$)}: The environmental stress tests reported in Table~\ref{tab:env_breakdown} (e.g., \textit{hospital} with $N=2$, \textit{night} with $N=6$) serve solely as qualitative failure mode probes rather than statistically asymptotic benchmarks. They demarcate failure boundaries (such as severe textureless shift or heavy fog) that require multi-domain diversity during pretraining.
    \item \textbf{Real-World Zero-Shot Domain Transfer}: As analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:public_transfer}, while Dioptra-DINO demonstrates strong cross-environment transfer within the TartanAir simulator and maintains optical equivariance across synthetic FOVs, zero-shot transfer onto real-world indoor domains (NYU Depth V2 indoor rooms) incurs an expected metric scale shift ($1.28\times$ on NYUv2). This confirms that geometric camera-ray conditioning resolves physical optical projection ambiguities, but cannot induce out-of-distribution semantic scale priors without diverse real-world multi-dataset pretraining.
    \item \textbf{Simulation to Physical Robotics Deployment}: All primary benchmark evaluations in this work are conducted on photorealistic simulation trajectories from TartanAir and offline evaluation suites. Evaluating physical closed-loop zero-shot transfer onto real micro-aerial vehicles (MAVs) equipped with embedded compute (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano at 10W) and noisy consumer rolling-shutter cameras represents an essential next step for real-world robotics deployment.
\end{itemize}
