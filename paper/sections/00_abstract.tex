Dense metric depth estimation from a single image is critical for autonomous robotics, spatial computing, and aerial navigation. However, state-of-the-art metric foundation models rely on massive transformer backbones ($>300$\,M parameters, $>1$\,GB memory), precluding real-time deployment on compute-constrained edge robotics. Conversely, lightweight models trained from scratch frequently collapse into blurry distance blobs and suffer from severe focal-depth ambiguity. We present \textbf{Dioptra-DINO}, a compact, geometry-coupled architecture ($27.5$\,M parameters, $105.05$\,MB weights) that bridges the gap between foundation-scale representation and edge feasibility. Dioptra-DINO couples a self-supervised DINOv2-Small vision backbone with analytical projective camera geometry: (i)~\textit{Trivision Ray Positional Encoding}, modulating transformer token representations via affine Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) driven by continuous optical ray triplets; (ii)~\textit{Angular Residual Attention (ARA)}, a parameter-free geometric attention bias that penalizes divergent ray angles $\theta_{ij}$ to sharpen object silhouettes and structural depth edges; (iii)~\textit{Decoupled Log-Median Scale Loss}, decoupling 3D relative shape convergence from absolute physical metric scale calibration; (iv)~\textit{Multi-Scale 3D Virtual Normal Loss (VNL)}, enforcing planar architectural geometry; and (v)~\textit{Dynamic Optical Pinhole Zoom Augmentation}, synthesizing over $334$ Billion virtual focal configurations during training. On a strict zero-shot cross-environment benchmark of TartanAir (quarantined via MD5 hashing), Dioptra-DINO achieves \textbf{$\mathbf{92.2\%}$ $\delta_1$ accuracy} and \textbf{$0.125$ AbsRel} on daylight industrial structures, with an overall held-out $\delta_1$ of \textbf{$35.8\%$} in raw unaligned physical metres and a scale ratio of \textbf{$1.192$}. Multi-camera field-of-view sweeps ($50^\circ \text{--} 100^\circ$) demonstrate smooth optical equivariance with up to \textbf{$-69.1\%$ error reduction} under wide-angle ray unprojection. Operating at \textbf{$45.9$\,FPS} ($21.8$\,ms) on NVIDIA Tesla T4 and \textbf{$38.6$\,FPS} ($25.9$\,ms) on Apple Silicon GPU, Dioptra-DINO establishes that explicit geometric inductive biases allow a compact $27.5$\,M parameter model to deliver robust metric depth within an edge-compatible envelope. Code is available at \url{https://github.com/SeranomTheGreat/dioptra}.
