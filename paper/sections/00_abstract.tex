Dense metric depth estimation from a single monocular image is fundamental for autonomous robotics, micro-aerial vehicles (MAVs), and spatial computing. However, current state-of-the-art metric foundation models rely on massive transformer backbones ($>300$\,M parameters, $>1$\,GB memory), precluding real-time deployment on compute-constrained edge platforms. Conversely, lightweight models trained from scratch frequently collapse into blurry distance representations and suffer from severe focal-depth ambiguity. We present \textbf{Dioptra-DINO}, a compact, geometry-coupled architecture ($27.5$\,M parameters, $105.05$\,MB weights) that bridges the gap between foundation-scale representation and edge feasibility. Dioptra-DINO couples a self-supervised DINOv2-Small vision backbone with analytical projective camera geometry: (i)~\textit{Trivision Ray Positional Encoding}, modulating transformer token representations via affine Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) driven by continuous optical ray triplets; (ii)~\textit{Angular Residual Attention (ARA)}, a geometric attention bias that penalizes divergent ray angles to sharpen object silhouettes and structural depth boundaries; (iii)~\textit{Decoupled Log-Median Scale Loss}, decoupling 3D relative shape convergence from absolute physical metric scale calibration; (iv)~\textit{Multi-Scale 3D Virtual Normal Loss (VNL)}, enforcing planar architectural surface orientation; and (v)~\textit{Dynamic Optical Pinhole Zoom Augmentation}, enforcing camera-intrinsic equivariance through continuous focal sampling during training. On a held-out cross-environment benchmark of TartanAir partitioned via deterministic trajectory-level hashing, Dioptra-DINO achieves \textbf{$79.7\%$ aligned $\delta_1$ accuracy} ($0.1520$ AbsRel) on industrial corridors (with peak single-frame performance reaching $92.2\%$), and an overall held-out raw unaligned $\delta_1$ of \textbf{$35.8\%$} ($59.9\%$ aligned) with an absolute metric scale ratio of \textbf{$1.192$}. Multi-camera field-of-view sweeps ($50^\circ \text{--} 100^\circ$) demonstrate consistent optical equivariance, yielding up to \textbf{$-69.1\%$ error reduction} under wide-angle ray unprojection. Operating at \textbf{$45.9$\,FPS} ($21.8$\,ms) on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 and \textbf{$38.6$\,FPS} ($25.9$\,ms) on Apple Silicon GPU, Dioptra-DINO demonstrates that explicit geometric inductive biases enable a compact $27.5$\,M parameter model to deliver robust metric depth within an edge-compatible envelope.
