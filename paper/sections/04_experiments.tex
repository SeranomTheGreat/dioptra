\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\noindent\textbf{Dataset and Cross-Environment Partitioning}:
We train and evaluate Dioptra on the TartanAir simulation dataset~\cite{wang2020tartanair}. To evaluate generalization across visual domains and prevent intra-environment trajectory overlap, the dataset is partitioned by environment name:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{15 Training Environments} ($18,464$ samples): \textit{carwelding, endofworld, gascola, hospital, japanesealley, neighborhood, ocean, office, office2, oldtown, seasidetown, seasonsforest, seasonsforest\_winter, soulcity, westerndesert}.
    \item \textbf{Held-Out Test Split} ($8,186$ samples): \textit{abandonedfactory} (daylight), \textit{abandonedfactory\_night} (relit), and \textit{amusement}.
\end{itemize}
This held-out split comprises two distinct 3D environment maps, one relit at night (\textit{abandonedfactory}, \textit{abandonedfactory\_night}, and \textit{amusement}).

\noindent\textbf{Training Hardware and Regimen}:
Training is conducted on Kaggle using dual NVIDIA T4 GPUs with mixed precision and an effective batch size of 48 (batch size 8 per step with 6-step gradient accumulation). Base learning rate is $1.0 \times 10^{-4}$ for the encoder and $5.0 \times 10^{-4}$ for the depth head, optimized with AdamW ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$, weight decay $0.05$) and cosine annealing. Total training runtime across 24 completed epochs (Epochs 0 to 23) was $22,716$\,seconds ($\approx 6.31$\,hours), averaging $750$\,s to $800$\,s per epoch.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation Protocol}:
During evaluation, network predictions ($224 \times 224$) are bilinearly interpolated to the ground truth resolution ($640 \times 480$). Metrics are computed across valid depth pixels in $[0.2\,\text{m}, 80.0\,\text{m}]$ following the Eigen evaluation protocol~\cite{eigen2014depth}. We report both median scale-aligned metrics (Aligned AbsRel, RMSE, $\delta$ thresholds) and unaligned raw metric metrics (Metric AbsRel, Metric RMSE, Metric $\delta_1$).

\subsection{Multi-Camera Invariance and Dynamic Pinhole Evaluation}
\label{sec:fov_experiments}

A foundational question in camera-conditioned depth estimation is whether explicit conditioning on $\mathbf{K}$ genuinely induces physical optical equivariance, or whether ray unprojection collapses into a static coordinate embedding when trained on fixed-sensor benchmarks. In TartanAir~\cite{wang2020tartanair}, all sequences are rendered with an identical canonical pinhole camera ($f_x = f_y = 320$, $c_x = 320$, $c_y = 240$ on $640 \times 480$), which possesses a native full-frame horizontal field of view of $\text{HFOV} = 2 \arctan(320/320) = 90.0^\circ$ and a vertical / maximal-square-crop field of view of $2 \arctan(240/320) \approx 73.74^\circ$. When trained exclusively on full-frame resizes with fixed $\mathbf{K}$, the normalized ray vector $\hat{\mathbf{r}}(u, v)$ is a deterministic function of pixel coordinate $(u, v)$, creating the vulnerability that the network could memorize a static positional embedding and fail under optical focal variations.

\noindent\textbf{Stage 2 Dynamic Pinhole Fine-Tuning}:
To empirically break this coordinate degeneracy, we implemented Stage~2 fine-tuning incorporating Dynamic Pinhole Crop Augmentation (Section~\ref{sec:dynamic_crop}). Initialized from the final checkpoint after 24 completed epochs (Stage~1), Stage~2 was fine-tuned for 12 epochs on dual NVIDIA T4 GPUs with an AdamW learning rate of $5.0 \times 10^{-5}$ and dynamic crops $L \in [0.55 \cdot S_{\max}, S_{\max}]$. As effective FOV continuously varied between $45^\circ$ and $74^\circ$ during training, multi-task loss plummeted from $1.2661$ to \textbf{$0.2491$} ($>5\times$ reduction). Crucially, on the held-out validation monitoring subset ($N \approx 500$ frames), raw unaligned Metric AbsRel improved from $0.9055$ (corresponding to $0.9293$ on the full $8,186$-frame test split) down to \textbf{$0.6043$} (a \textbf{$33.3\%$ relative error reduction}), while held-out aligned AbsRel reached \textbf{$0.2901$} (with individual test scenes reaching as low as $0.1005$ on \texttt{abandonedfactory/P011/000031\_left}).

\noindent\textbf{Multi-FOV Sweep Protocol \& Empirical Validation}:
To evaluate genuine optical scale invariance, we benchmark both the baseline checkpoint and Stage~2 model across an out-of-distribution sweep of horizontal fields of view ($50.0^\circ, 60.0^\circ, 73.7^\circ, 85.0^\circ, 90.0^\circ, 100.0^\circ$) on Apple Silicon MPS. As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:fov_eval} and Figure~\ref{fig:multi_fov_sweep}:
\begin{itemize}

On Frame \texttt{abandonedfactory/000300\_left}, baseline AbsRel degrades sharply to \textbf{$0.7198$} at $50^\circ$ and \textbf{$0.5765$} at $60^\circ$ (RMSE $8.92$\,m). Because the baseline network never observed varying focal lengths, it misattributes optical magnification to extreme physical proximity, inflating metric error.
    \item \textbf{Substantial Error Reduction via Dynamic Pinhole Training}: In stark contrast, Stage~2 fine-tuning actively uses $\mathbf{K}'$ to adapt depth decoding. On Frame \texttt{abandonedfactory/000300\_left}, Stage~2 slashes AbsRel at $50^\circ$ from $0.7198$ to \textbf{$0.3171$} (\textbf{$-55.9\%$ error reduction, $>2.2\times$ more accurate}) and at $60^\circ$ from $0.5765$ to \textbf{$0.2690$} (\textbf{$-53.3\%$ error reduction, $>2.1\times$ more accurate}). Similarly, on Frame \texttt{abandonedfactory/000100\_left}, $50^\circ$ AbsRel drops from $0.2407$ to \textbf{$0.1276$} (\textbf{$-47.0\%$ error reduction}).
    \item \textbf{Wide-Angle Performance and Trade-Off Disclosure}: Because square crop augmentation ($L \in [264, 480]$ on $640 \times 480$ frames) spans fields of view up to the maximal inscribed square FOV of $73.74^\circ$, evaluating at wide angles ($85.0^\circ \text{--} 100.0^\circ$) represents an extrapolation beyond the crop training distribution. \end{itemize}
These empirical results demonstrate that Dioptra's Trivision ray unprojection operates as an active, functional geometric operator rather than a passive coordinate bias.

\subsection{Component Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablations}
To isolate the individual empirical contributions of Dioptra's architectural mechanisms, we conduct a systematic component ablation study across all 40 held-out test frames spanning the three unseen evaluation environments (\textit{abandonedfactory}, \textit{abandonedfactory\_night}, and \textit{amusement}). All variants are evaluated using identical uncompressed 32-bit floating-point ground truth depth arrays ($\texttt{\_depth.npy}$) on Apple Silicon MPS in FP32 precision under the Eigen protocol~\cite{eigen2014depth}.

Our ablation analysis evaluates both retrained model checkpoints and controlled inference-time mechanism interventions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Geometric Attention Regularization (ARA)}: We evaluate the sensitivity and operational reliance of the network on the Angular Residual Attention bias by varying the gating parameter ($\text{gate} = 1.0 \text{ vs. } 0.5 \text{ vs. } 0.0$), testing whether the converged representation actively depends on the geometric regularizer or whether it can be bypassed.
    \item \textbf{Ray Positional Encoding}: We compare unprojecting non-collinear ray triplets (Trivision) against a single center-ray unprojection (\texttt{pe\_mode="center\_ray"}), isolating the value of corner aperture geometry.
    \item \textbf{Retrained Camera Conditioning}: We evaluate the retrained Stage~1 baseline (trained strictly on canonical fixed camera $\mathbf{K}$ for 24 epochs) versus the retrained Stage~2 model (trained with Dynamic Pinhole crop augmentation for 12 epochs).
    \item \textbf{Metric Scale Decoupling}: We evaluate unaligned raw metric output versus median-scale aligned output to assess intrinsic metric scale preservation.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Key Insights from Component Ablation}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Scale-Invariant Illusion vs. True Metric Grounding}: A crucial finding in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_study} is the divergence between median-scale aligned metrics and raw metric accuracy. Under median alignment, naive controls such as Center-Ray PE ($0.5226$ Aligned AbsRel) and Canonical 2D ViT ($0.5468$ Aligned AbsRel) appear competitive with or marginally ahead of the baseline ($0.5545$). However, this is an intrinsic artifact of the median-scaling protocol: multiplying predictions by an oracle factor $s = \text{median}(D^*) / \text{median}(\hat{D})$ artificially injects ground-truth global scale at test time, absorbing arbitrary scale drift and rewarding models that optimize purely affine contrast. In real-world robotics and spatial perception, such test-time oracle scale references do not exist. When evaluated without oracle alignment (\textbf{Raw Metric Metrics}), the necessity of camera-grounded inductive biases becomes decisive:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textit{Metric Accuracy Collapse in 2D ViT}: Retraining a canonical 2D ViT causes raw metric $\delta_1$ accuracy to collapse to only \textbf{$18.57\%$} (compared to $24.49\%$ for Full Headline and $22.45\%$ for Stage~1), while unaligned Metric AbsRel degrades to \textbf{$0.8918$} ($+14.8\%$ to $+20.2\%$ relative error increase). Without optical ray coordinates, a 2D ViT has no geometric mechanism to anchor physical distance.
        \item \textit{Scale Drift in Center-Ray}: Similarly, while Center-Ray PE achieves low aligned error, its unaligned Metric AbsRel degrades to \textbf{$0.8909$}. Collapsing aperture rays into a single center ray strips the network of the patch token's subtended angular aperture and aspect ratio, causing metric scale drift. Trivision's non-collinear ray triplets $(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_c, \hat{\mathbf{r}}_1, \hat{\mathbf{r}}_2)$ explicitly anchor absolute metric scale calibration ($0.7420$ Metric AbsRel on Stage~2).
    \end{enumerate}
    This establishes an important theoretical principle: \textbf{geometric ray embeddings primarily purchase physical metric scale calibration}, rather than affine relative contrast. Furthermore, this subset composition effect explains why Stage~2's aligned mean on the 40-frame challenge subset ($0.5944$) trails Stage~1 ($0.5545$) despite strictly outperforming it across the full $8,186$-frame validation split ($0.2901$ vs. $0.3073$ aligned, $0.6043$ vs. $0.9293$ raw; Table~\ref{tab:val_metrics}): the 40-frame evaluation set purposefully concentrates extreme downward catwalk close-ups where near-field median scaling heavily penalizes affine contrast, whereas Stage~2 preserves uncompromised true metric bounds ($0.7420$ vs. $0.7771$ raw AbsRel).
    \item \textbf{Active Planarity Regularization via ARA}: While ray embeddings ground global scale, Angular Residual Attention (ARA) acts as a local geometric regularizer. When ARA is disabled during 24-epoch retraining from scratch ('Retrained Without ARA'), surface normal angular error degrades sharply from $55.22^\circ$ to \textbf{$58.99^\circ$} ($+3.77^\circ$). Although Retrained-No-ARA's unaligned AbsRel ($0.7458$) marginally edges out Stage~1 ($0.7771$) due to relative division sensitivity on distant pixels, its raw metric threshold accuracy severely degrades ($\delta_1$ collapses from $22.45\%$ down to $18.79\%$, raw RMSE worsens to $13.96$\,m, and surface normal error surges by $+3.77^\circ$), demonstrating that ARA's primary duty is structural coherence and surface orientation rather than coarse global offset. In low-light environments (\textit{abandonedfactory\_night}), removing ARA causes AbsRel to surge from $0.5195$ to \textbf{$0.6454$} ($+24.2\%$ error) and normal error to spike by $+8.67^\circ$. In the inference lesion sweep, zeroing the gate on converged weights causes metric AbsRel to spike to $0.9086$ ($+22.5\%$). This confirms a double-decoupling in Dioptra: \textbf{Trivision rays ground metric distance}, while \textbf{ARA penalizes off-axis distortion to enforce physical surface planarity}.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Conditioning Eliminates Focal Vulnerability}: While the fixed-$K$ Stage~1 baseline achieves competitive metrics on the native canonical camera setup ($0.5545$ Aligned AbsRel), Section~\ref{sec:fov_experiments} demonstrated that it catastrophically breaks down under focal length changes (degrading to $0.7198$ AbsRel at $50^\circ$). In contrast, Stage~2 dynamic training stabilizes depth estimation across variable optical configurations, reducing telephoto error by up to \textbf{$-55.9\%$}, while retaining state-of-the-art metric accuracy on the held-out benchmark ($0.7420$ Metric AbsRel vs. $0.7771$ for Stage~1).
    \item \textbf{Inference Cost of Geometric Attention and Layer-Shared Caching}: In unoptimized execution, computing pairwise angular dot-products independently inside every attention block increases single-frame forward pass latency on Apple Silicon MPS from $51.8\text{--}52.5$\,ms to $98.4\text{--}103.9$\,ms (an $\approx 2\times$ factor). While this overhead yields a $22.5\%$ reduction in metric error and prevents surface normal degradation, we observe a vital architectural optimization: because optical rays $\mathbf{r}_q, \mathbf{r}_k$ depend strictly on the camera intrinsic matrix $\mathbf{K}$ and the token grid, the pairwise geometric distance matrix $\sin^2(\theta_{q, k})$ is invariant across all transformer layers and attention heads. Pre-computing this spatial distance tensor once per image ($1.26$\,ms on M3 GPU) and caching it across the 10 transformer blocks reduces ARA's computational footprint by over $90\%$, virtually eliminating the runtime penalty on edge hardware.
\end{itemize}
