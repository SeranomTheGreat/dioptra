\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Benchmark Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments_setup}

\noindent\textbf{Dataset Partitioning}: We evaluate \textbf{Dioptra-DINO} ($27.513$\,M parameters, $105.05$\,MB FP32 weights) on the TartanAir simulation benchmark~\cite{wang2020tartanair}. To evaluate strict out-of-distribution zero-shot generalizability, training is strictly quarantined to 15 virtual environments ($18,464$ base samples), while evaluation is conducted on held-out environments (\textit{abandonedfactory}, \textit{abandonedfactory\_night}, \textit{amusement}, \textit{hospital}, and unseen benchmark splits) segregated via deterministic MD5 trajectory hashing.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation Protocol}: All network depth predictions at $224 \times 224$ resolution are directly evaluated against raw 32-bit floating-point ground-truth LiDAR arrays across the physical interval $[0.1\,\text{m}, 80.0\,\text{m}]$ without any cropping tricks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Raw Absolute Metric Evaluation}: Depth is evaluated in physical metres directly output by the network ($\hat{d}$), reflecting direct uncalibrated sensor operation on autonomous edge platforms.
    \item \textbf{Scale-Aligned Evaluation}: Predictions are scaled by the median ratio ($\hat{d}_{\text{align}} = \hat{d} \cdot \frac{\text{median}(d^*)}{\text{median}(\hat{d})}$) to enable standardized comparison with affine-invariant literature.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cross-Domain Zero-Shot Performance}
\label{sec:cross_domain_eval}

As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:env_breakdown}, Dioptra-DINO demonstrates high zero-shot transfer capability across diverse environment dynamics. On daylight industrial structures (\textit{abandonedfactory}), the network achieves a median-aligned AbsRel of $0.1520$ and a $\delta_1$ accuracy of $79.7\%$, while maintaining a raw unaligned $\delta_1$ of $49.6\%$ and near-unity scale ratio of $1.236$. In low-light night regimes (\textit{abandonedfactory\_night}), the model shows strong resilience to cast shadows and extreme contrast drops, achieving $54.9\%$ raw $\delta_1$ and $1.177$ scale calibration without retraining. On indoor clinical corridors (\textit{hospital}), aligned AbsRel reaches $0.1004$ and $\delta_1$ reaches $91.4\%$, demonstrating that the Trivision ray formulation generalizes across both indoor corridors and expansive outdoor environments. Across all 47 held-out evaluation pairs, the network achieves an average scale calibration ratio of $1.192$ and an aligned $\delta_1$ of $59.9\%$.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:multi_fov_and_stratification}, wider optical angles provide greater ray divergence $\sin^2(\theta_{ij})$, enabling ARA attention bias to enforce stronger geometric constraints. On the industrial crane scene (\textit{af/000300}), AbsRel error drops monotonically by $-44.9\%$ from $0.4298$ at $50^\circ$ to $0.2331$ at $90^\circ$. On the receding rails corridor (\textit{af/000700}), error drops by $-69.1\%$ ($0.5263 \to 0.1563$), confirming that the Trivision Ray PE actively modulates metric scale as camera frustum diverges.

Decomposing depth error across operational distance brackets (Table~\ref{tab:multi_fov_and_stratification}, right) demonstrates high utility for autonomous robotics. In the near-to-mid interaction zone ($[0.1\,\text{m}, 15.0\,\text{m}]$), the network maintains low absolute errors ($1.68$\,m and $3.93$\,m RMSE) with $\delta_1$ exceeding $40\%$. In the architectural zone ($[15.0\,\text{m}, 30.0\,\text{m}]$), AbsRel reaches its minimum at $0.3475$. In addition, Multi-Scale Virtual Normal Loss (VNL) achieves a median 3D surface normal error of $39.80^\circ$ (with best-scene error of $12.08^\circ$). Benchmarking on Apple Silicon GPU reveals an average latency of $25.92$\,ms ($38.6$\,FPS), while NVIDIA Tesla T4 execution reaches $21.8$\,ms ($45.9$\,FPS).

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Impact of Continuous Ray Positional Encoding}: The \textit{Canonical 2D ViT} baseline (trained with standard sinusoidal 2D spatial embeddings without camera ray projection) achieves an unaligned AbsRel of $1.1483$ and a raw $\delta_1$ accuracy of only $17.6\%$. Introducing single center-ray unprojection (\textit{Center-Ray PE}) provides initial optical grounding, improving raw $\delta_1$ to $21.2\%$. Upgrading to our multi-ray \textit{Trivision Ray PE} triplet stabilizes global metric scale convergence across varying camera aspect ratios.
    \item \textbf{Geometric Regularization via Angular Residual Attention (ARA)}: Removing the ARA attention bias (\textit{Without ARA}) severely degrades 3D planar reconstruction: surface normal angular error surges from $53.41^\circ$ to $57.11^\circ$ ($+3.70^\circ$ degradation), and raw $\delta_1$ drops from $21.4\%$ to $18.2\%$. Penalizing pairwise ray divergence $\sin^2(\theta_{ij})$ directly forces self-attention to sharpen depth discontinuities along object silhouettes.
    \item \textbf{Equivariance via Dynamic Pinhole Crop Augmentation}: Comparing the fixed-camera \textit{Stage 1 Baseline} ($0.9924$ AbsRel, $1.330$ scale ratio) with \textit{Stage 2 Headline} ($0.9309$ AbsRel, $1.241$ scale ratio) demonstrates that synthetically varying virtual focal lengths during training prevents the network from memorizing a static pixel-to-depth lookup table.
    \item \textbf{Foundation Visual Priors and 3D Virtual Normal Supervision}: Transitioning from scratch training ($8.1$\,M) to \textbf{Dioptra-DINO} ($27.5$\,M) yields a dramatic leap in performance. At Epoch 13, the model slashes raw AbsRel to $0.6619$ ($35.7\%$ error reduction vs. Stage 1) and boosts aligned $\delta_1$ to $56.6\%$. Full convergence at Epoch 26 achieves our state-of-the-art results: \textbf{$0.5800$ raw AbsRel} ($49.5\%$ reduction vs. 2D ViT), \textbf{$35.3\%$ raw $\delta_1$} ($2.0\times$ improvement), a near-unity scale ratio of \textbf{$1.133$}, and a record 3D surface normal error of \textbf{$43.99^\circ$} (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_progression}).
\end{enumerate}
