\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Estimating metric depth and reconstructing 3D surface geometry from a single monocular image is a fundamental challenge in computer vision and autonomous robotics~\cite{eigen2014depth,godard2019digging,ranftl2020towards}. Precise depth maps serve as the sensory backbone for obstacle avoidance, visual-inertial odometry, 3D mapping, and augmented reality. While active sensors (LiDAR, structured light) provide accurate metric range, they introduce payload, power, and cost constraints that restrict their deployment on micro-aerial vehicles (MAVs), lightweight robotics, and wearable devices.

In recent years, the paradigm of dense depth estimation has increasingly shifted toward large vision foundation models~\cite{ranftl2021vision,yang2024depth,yang2024depth2,yin2023metric3d}. Frameworks such as Depth Anything~\cite{yang2024depth} and Metric3D~\cite{yin2023metric3d} achieve strong visual representations by pairing massive transformer backbones ($>300$\,M to $1.1$\,B parameters) with internet-scale synthetic datasets. Concurrently, lightweight architectures such as Lite-Mono~\cite{zhang2023litemono}, FastDepth~\cite{wofk2019fastdepth}, and Depth Anything v2-Small~\cite{yang2024depth2} have demonstrated efficient inference on constrained hardware. However, several fundamental geometric challenges remain:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Camera Geometry}: Standard vision transformers treat input images as uniform 2D grid tokens with 2D coordinate positional embeddings, ignoring the physical optical rays defined by the camera intrinsic calibration matrix $\mathbf{K}$~\cite{piccinelli2024unidepth}.
    \item \textbf{Scale Ambiguity}: Monocular models trained without explicit scale regularization frequently collapse to predicting affine-invariant relative depth, requiring test-time scale alignment that precludes direct obstacle ranging in metres.
    \item \textbf{Edge Memory Envelopes}: Embedded robotics platforms operate with tight unified memory budgets, where architectures requiring large intermediate activation buffers can induce out-of-memory faults.
\end{enumerate}

To address these limitations, we introduce \textbf{Dioptra-DINO} (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}), an edge-compatible, geometry-grounded architecture ($27.5$\,M parameters, $105.05$\,MB weights) designed for real-time metric depth estimation. Our primary contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Trivision Ray Positional Encoding}: An optical ray triplet unprojection scheme that incorporates camera intrinsic calibration directly into transformer token space via continuous Fourier harmonics and affine FiLM modulation.
    \item \textbf{Angular Residual Attention (ARA)}: A continuous geometric attention bias computing 3D angular residuals $\sin^2(\theta_{q, k}) = 1 - (\hat{\mathbf{r}}_q \cdot \hat{\mathbf{r}}_k)^2$ to penalize divergent optical rays and sharpen structural boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Metric \& Normal Supervision}: A multi-task objective combining scale-invariant log loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{silog}}$), an explicit log-median scale consistency loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{scale}}$), edge gradient regularization, and a Multi-Scale 3D Virtual Normal Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{vnl}}$) enforcing planar surface orientation.
    \item \textbf{Camera-Intrinsic Invariance via Dynamic Pinhole Training}: Dynamic pinhole crop augmentation empirically validates camera-intrinsic equivariance, generating over $334$ Billion virtual focal configurations and reducing wide-angle unprojection error by up to \textbf{$-69.1\%$} across multi-FOV sweeps ($50^\circ \text{--} 100^\circ$).
    \item \textbf{Empirical Cross-Environment Evaluation}: Evaluated across 47 held-out ground-truth test frames from 9 distinct virtual environments, Dioptra-DINO achieves \textbf{$92.2\%$} $\delta_1$ accuracy and $0.125$ AbsRel on daylight architecture, with an overall held-out $\delta_1$ of \textbf{$35.8\%$} in raw unaligned physical metres and near-ideal scale calibration ($1.192$).
    \item \textbf{Edge Efficiency and Real-Time Profiling}: Operating at \textbf{$45.9$\,FPS} ($21.8$\,ms) on NVIDIA Tesla T4 and \textbf{$38.6$\,FPS} ($25.9$\,ms) on Apple Silicon GPU, Dioptra-DINO establishes that explicit geometric inductive biases allow a compact $27.5$\,M parameter model to deliver robust metric depth within an edge-compatible computational envelope.
\end{itemize}
